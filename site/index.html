for radio stations

Hello. That was Yonder.

If you followed a link in your server logs, you've found the right page. Here's what Yonder is doing when it plays or records your station.

what you'll see

When someone listens live:

Yonder/0.9.13 (+https://mattsapps.github.io/yonder/)

When someone records one of your shows:

Yonder/0.9.13 (radio recording; +https://mattsapps.github.io/yonder/; listener 3fk9q2xa)

A recording is one listener asking Yonder to save your show so they can hear it later, for example at the same local time in another time zone. Each recording is that one person's own listening.

the listener tag is there for you

We put the listener tag there expressly for your convenience. We want you to have accurate figures for your listenership. We want Yonder to help your shows grow.

Lots of listeners can share one internet address, especially on mobile networks, so without the tag many Yonder listeners could look like one. With it, you can count them properly:

  • Every recording schedule gets its own random tag.
  • The next airing of the same show carries the same tag, so a regular listener shows up as one regular listener.
  • The tag isn't linked to the person, their phone or anything else they listen to. Another station never sees the same tag.

recordings stay with the listener

Recordings are encrypted so that they can only be played through the phone that recorded them. They can't be shared and they can't be copied.

  • Each recording plays once, then deletes itself.
  • If it hasn't been played, it deletes itself when the next airing is recorded, or 25 hours after recording finished, whichever comes first.

why we do it this way

It's the same reason Yonder doesn't skip the adverts in podcasts, even though it could. We want radio stations and podcasts to stay profitable. We love them.
